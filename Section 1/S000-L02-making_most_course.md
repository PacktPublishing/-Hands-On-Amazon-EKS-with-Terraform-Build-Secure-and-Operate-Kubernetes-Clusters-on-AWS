Hello everyone 👋 I'm super happy to have you here, and before we get started, let me give you a couple of tips to make the most of this course 😊

This course is hands-on, so you will get the most from it by building the labs yourself, not just watching the lectures. Here are a couple of habits that will help you to make good progress:

- **Do the labs yourself.** When a lab starts, pause the video, open your editor, and build it before you watch me do it. I provide high-level lab descriptions on purpose: I want you to develop the skill of searching for information, writing code, and debugging errors 💪 Otherwise, you will just develop the skill of copy-pasting 🤷
- **Try before you check the solution.** You can find the solution code in the Git repos, but I recommend trying the lab first, and then comparing your version against the solution to see where and why they differ. It might be that your solution is better and more robust than mine, and that's why you should try it yourself 😊
- **Work through the course in order.** Most of the labs build on each other: each one picks up where the last left off and builds on it. Skipping ahead means starting from a state you never created, so go straight through.
- **Treat the small AWS cost as an investment.** The course runs on real AWS, so it costs a few real dollars. If you have no free credits available anymore, consider this as an investment in learning in a real-world environment. That is the price of practicing on the actual platform instead of a simulator! Tear the infrastructure down at the end of each session to keep the cost small.
- **Watch the announcements.** New sections will be added over time and posted in the course announcements. Check them now and then so you know when a new topic is available.

Do these to get the most out of this course and develop relevant, widely applicable skills that go way beyond just writing an EKS module definition! I hope you enjoy the course, and I look forward to receiving your feedback!
